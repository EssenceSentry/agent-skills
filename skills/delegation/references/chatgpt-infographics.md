## Prepare the information, then the visual direction

Ground the brief in the actual source and explain the technical story self-containedly. Explicitly request **image generation**, either one image or a coordinated series. Do not merely ask for infographic advice or a textual layout.

Put the full information to convey before aesthetic instructions. Include what the particular explanation needs: purpose and audience, key concepts, module responsibilities, inputs and outputs, data flow, relationships, algorithm steps, equations and symbol meanings, or a concrete worked example. Preserve meaningful distinctions and assumptions. Supply exact labels or equations where fidelity matters. Distinguish source facts from illustrative examples; do not let visual simplification invent behavior.

Only after the content, describe composition, visual hierarchy, typography, palette, illustration style, dimensions or aspect ratio, intended viewing size, and any supplied design references. Keep this direction specific to the user's intended artifact. Ask for multiple images when a single image would force tiny text or omit essential information; use consistent notation, colors, and visual language across the series.

> Generate [one detailed infographic image / a coordinated series of infographic images] explaining [subject] for [audience].

> [Describe the desired composition, hierarchy, typography, colors, visual style, size, and viewing context. For a series, specify the shared visual language. Keep text and equations legible at the intended display size.]
